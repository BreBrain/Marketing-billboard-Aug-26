# Pura OOH creative brief: the breadth problem

Built from the 52 entry reference collection in this folder. Open `Pura-OOH-Superapp-Moodboard.html` alongside this document.

---

## 1. Method and coverage

62 image searches through Firecrawl produced 1,240 raw results. A pre download filter (minimum 800px wide, landscape aspect) cut that to 524 downloaded candidates. Every one of the 524 was reviewed by eye, shortlisting 52, and every one of the 52 was then audited a second time at full resolution. That audit removed 6 and demoted 6.

**Final set: 46 entries. 20 Tier 1, 26 Tier 2. Only 14 are both Tier 1 and a photographed real placement.**

**The target was 30 to 40 strict examples and the honest result is 20.** That is the first finding, not a shortfall. Searching brand after brand for billboards returns placement photography in which the app is almost never shown. The layout Brenton described is far more common in agency renders and stock templates than on real streets.

**Two cautions on the evidence.** The second audit removed three AI generated "billboards" that had passed the first triage: an Uber airport scene listing "Rent" twice and captioned "Explore Things a do", a bank board reading "Mobile Roseting" and "GET IT IS Google Play", and a telecom composite. They were convincing at thumbnail size. Anything sourced this way needs checking at full size before it informs a decision.

**What this does not cover.** No Behance or awards gallery scrape was run, so the craft ceiling is under represented. No paid archive (Coloribus, Adforum premium) was accessed. Watermarked stock was rejected rather than licensed. Campaign years are often unrecoverable from image search, so many entries carry `unknown`.

## 2. What the 52 actually show

### The message is short, and shorter than you expect

Message length runs from 3 to 26 words, median 7. Every line that works at roadside distance sits at the bottom of that range:

- "The everything app." (Careem, 3 words)
- "Go anywhere. Get anything." (Uber, 4)
- "LIVE EASY! All in one app" (Yango, 5)
- "Si tienes Rappi, tienes todo" (Rappi, 5)
- "Cualquier cosa, por Nequi" (Nequi, 4)

The long entries are all trade press or web assets, not real roadside placements.

### There are two opposing strategies, and they do not mix

**Assert breadth.** Say you do everything, show nothing. Careem, Rappi, Nequi, Zomato ("the only food app you'll ever need"), Yango Play ("WATCH. LISTEN. PLAY. IN ONE SUPERAPP"). Cheap to read, high on confidence, zero on proof.

**Demonstrate breadth.** Show the range on the right. Yango's three labelled tiles, Uber's two column word list, Swiggy's A to Z grid, Grab's three phones, Monzo's and Alipay's four screens.

No entry in the set does both well. The boards that try to assert *and* enumerate become crowded and lose the roadside read.

### Breadth devices ranked by how well they survive distance

| Device | Count | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Typographic enumeration (a word list, no UI) | Uber | **Best.** Uber's "Go anywhere. Get anything." flanked by subway / scooter / ride / train / bike / moped and courier / cleaner / food / groceries / dog walker / meds is the strongest breadth communication in the entire set, and it contains no app screen at all |
| Three or four labelled tiles or screens | 9 (Yango, Grab, Monzo, Alipay) | **Works.** The ceiling is four. Yango's RIDES / FOOD / DELIVERY is the cleanest |
| One dense screen with a single dominant element | 24 | **Works only if one thing dominates.** Paytm's scanner and Yandex's map read instantly because each screen has one hero object |
| Product grid used as texture | Swiggy A to Z | **Works,** because it is not meant to be read. It registers as "lots of things" and stops there |
| Large icon grid | Grab's 18 service "A PEEK AT OUR INVENTORY" | **Fails at distance.** Legible as a print or digital asset, illegible from a road |

### The template finding

Stock billboard mockup templates sold to designers are, almost without exception, message-left / phone-right. The layout Brenton described is the *default convention* of app advertising as taught by templates. Combined with how rarely it appears in real photographed placements, that produces the sharpest observation in the study: **this layout is what app advertising looks like in mockups, and not what it looks like on the street.** Adopting it means joining a convention that lives mostly in portfolios.

### The GCC reality is different

15 GCC entries, the largest regional block, and the strong local brands deliberately avoid the app screen. Careem asserts ("The everything app."). talabat shows food and a car. noon shows product and a numeral. Yango dropped the app screen for its Dubai board while keeping it for Lusaka. The only GCC entries that hold Tier 1 are Bolt and the Vodafone Cash Egypt set. GoChat, Wio and Careem Plus were all demoted on the second audit for having no legible app screen.

That pattern is itself the answer to whether this format is a GCC norm. It is not.

Two GCC specific constraints show up in the evidence:

1. **Format.** Dubai's dominant inventory is very wide and very short (bridge banners, Sheikh Zayed unipoles). noon's and talabat's boards are effectively letterbox. A tall phone render fights that ratio. Yango's Dubai board dropped the app screen entirely and used a product lockup.
2. **Bilingual inverts the layout.** In the Arabic executions (Vodafone Cash, Careem Plus) the phone sits **left** and the message **right**, because Arabic reads right to left. "Message left, app screen right" is a Latin script convention. The Arabic version of any Pura board is its mirror, not a translation dropped into the same frame.

## 3. The gap

**Every breadth demonstration in the collection is commerce. Not one is health.**

Rides, food, groceries, parcels, payments, streaming. The claim "we do everything" is easy in commerce because more choice is straightforwardly good news. Health is the one category where a fanned out grid of services carries the opposite meaning: seven clinical service icons on a hoarding reads as *seven things wrong with you*, or as burden and complexity. Grab's 18 icon inventory board is the failure mode, and a Pura service grid (PureScore, care plans, consultations, pharmacy, nutrition, wearables, AI agent) would land closer to that board than to Yango's three tiles.

---

## 4. The Pura execution

### The strategic move

Do not fan the services out. **Show them converging.**

Every super app in this set uses breadth to mean *more choice*. Pura's own users have already said the opposite is what they want. The focus group finding on record is *"I don't need more data, I need better decisions."* A board that enumerates Pura's services argues against that finding. A board that shows many inputs producing one answer argues for it, and no one else in the collection is making that argument.

### The left side

Short, per the evidence. Three to six words. It should promise a decision, not a service count, and it must avoid the two failure modes already logged for Pura copy: no character coaching, no hype.

Directions to develop and test, not final lines:

- Everything considered. One answer.
- All of you, in one number.
- Your whole health, decided.

Route the final line through `pura-brand-brain` and `pura-ux-writing` before anything goes to a designer. Pura punctuation rules apply: no em dashes, en dashes or hyphens; British English; AED; "users" not "members".

### The right side

**One screen, one dominant element: PureScore.** Follow the Paytm and Yandex pattern, where a single hero object carries the screen, not the Grab inventory pattern.

Breadth is communicated by what visibly *feeds* the score, not by a service menu. Three or four contributing inputs at most, per the four device ceiling in the evidence, rendered as small feeding elements around the score rather than as a navigation grid. The viewer should understand "many things went into this one number" without being asked to read what they are.

**Test the no screen route as a genuine equal, not a fallback.** Three separate findings point the same way. The Uber word list is the strongest breadth communication in the collection and contains no app screen. The GCC's strongest brands have all chosen assertion or product over interface. And the split layout turns out to live mainly in mockups rather than on real streets. A Pura board carrying the line flanked by a short typographic list of what Pura draws on would survive Dubai's letterbox formats, where a portrait phone render will not.

### Production notes from the evidence

- Build the Arabic execution as a **mirror**, phone left and message right, not as a translation poured into the Latin layout.
- Check the artwork against a very wide, very short crop before approving it. That is the dominant Dubai format and it is where a portrait phone render breaks.
- Contrast for night. The Paytm G20 board and the Bolt board both work after dark because the screen is bright against a dark field.

---

## 5. What to decide

1. **Assert or demonstrate.** Careem already owns pure assertion in this market with "The everything app." Demonstrating is the differentiated move and the harder craft problem.
2. **Screen or no screen.** On the evidence this is the real decision, and it is closer than the original brief assumed. The strongest breadth board in the set (Uber) uses no app screen, every strong GCC brand avoids one, and the split layout is largely a mockup convention. Run both routes.
3. **Whether the layout is even the right target.** The brief that started this work assumed message-left / app-screen-right was the thing to emulate. The collection only partly supports that. It is a real and legible convention, but it is not what the best local or global work is doing.
4. **Whether this goes further.** Turning this into campaign copy and channel adaptation is `marketing-conductor` work. This brief stops at the creative direction.
