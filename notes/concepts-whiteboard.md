# Answering the whiteboard

Five variations on the sketch, ordered from **the most direct reading to the most abstract**.

The sketch has three things in both panels: **the phone is the central object**, the entities are **named and specific** (Cardiologist, Endo, Gyno / hospital, clinic, pharmacy), and the **connections are visible**. The first pass of concepts substituted mood for that mechanic. These do not.

The craft problem is that the moodboard measured labelled diagrams at 36% billboard viability. The resolution is to **replace text labels with things that read instantly**: a cardiologist in scrubs is the label, a green cross is the label. Then make the connections physical light rather than thin lines.

## The range, and how to use it

| | Register | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| W3 In and Out | most direct | People converge in on the left, places radiate out on the right, phone between them. The whole sketch in one frame |
| W1 Specialists In | direct | The left panel. Three specialists, three threads of light, one phone in a hand |
| W2 Places Out | direct | The right panel. The phone projecting to hospital, clinic and pharmacy |
| W4 The Prism | abstract | The mechanic as optics. Many threads in, one clean beam out, through a phone shaped piece of glass |
| W5 The Field | most abstract | No objects at all. One bright point, everything converging and flowing out again |

The direct end explains the proposition to someone who has never heard of Pura. The abstract end is brand, and works once the proposition is known. A launch sequence would run direct first and abstract later.

**Known flaw.** W2 and W3 carry small garbled lettering on the model clinic signage, an artefact of model rendered text. It is illegible at billboard distance and the buildings read correctly by form and colour, but it would need cleaning before production.
