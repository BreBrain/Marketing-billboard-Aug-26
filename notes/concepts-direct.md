# The direct set

Brenton's note: *people need it explained. Labels or obvious symbols, direct, not implied.*

He is right, and the earlier caution was based on my own misreading of the evidence.

## The correction

I had been treating "labelled" as a risk, on the basis that labelled diagrams scored 36% billboard viability in the 80 reference moodboard. That is not what the data says. **The 36% was about small type in consultancy diagrams**, not about labelling as such.

The two strongest breadth communications in the entire out of home study were both pure labels:

- **Yango**, three large tiles reading RIDES, FOOD, DELIVERY. Billboard viable.
- **Uber**, a two column word list of services either side of the line. Recorded as the strongest breadth communication found anywhere in the collection.

Both label everything. Both work. **The failure mode is small type, not labels.** A big label is a picture.

## The rule for this set

1. **Name the thing.** Large, bold, capitalised, high contrast.
2. **Add the obvious symbol** beside it. A heart for cardiology. A green cross for pharmacy. An H for hospital.
3. **Draw the connection thickly.** A fat line of light, not a hairline.
4. **Three items, never more.** Yango's ceiling, confirmed across the collection.
5. **Nothing implied.** If a viewer has to work it out, it has failed.

## D1  Labelled Specialists

Three doctors, each under a large white label and a large white symbol: **CARDIOLOGY** with a heart, **DIABETES** with a blood droplet, **WOMEN'S HEALTH** with a female symbol. Three thick blue lines run down and converge into a phone held in the hand.

> # All your specialists, in one place.
> Cardiology, diabetes, women's health. Every specialist, one app.
>
> Pura knows your body like you do.

This is the whiteboard's left panel with the labels actually written on, exactly as the sketch does.

## D2  Labelled Places

Three buildings, each under a large white label and carrying its own sign: **HOSPITAL** with a lit H, **CLINIC** with a white cross, **PHARMACY** with a green cross. Three thick blue beams run from a phone in the foreground out to each.

> # Every clinic and pharmacy, connected.
> Book the hospital, see the clinic, collect from the pharmacy. One app.
>
> Pura knows your body like you do.

The whiteboard's right panel, written on.

## D3  The Whole Map

Both halves in one frame. Phone large in the centre. On the left, two clinicians under the label **YOUR SPECIALISTS**, with light running inward. On the right, a hospital and a pharmacy under the label **YOUR CARE NETWORK**, with light running outward.

> # Everyone you need. Everywhere you go.
> Specialists in, care out. All of it from one app.
>
> Pura knows your body like you do.

Two labels only, so it stays readable at speed while still explaining itself. **This is the recommended lead execution.**

## Where the earlier work now sits

| Set | Register | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **D1 to D3** | Direct, fully labelled | **Lead.** Launch, cold audiences, anyone who has never heard of Pura |
| W1 to W3 | Direct, symbols only, no labels | Second wave, once the proposition is known |
| W4, W5 | Abstract, no objects | Brand layer only. Not for explaining anything |
| B1 to B5, V1 to V5 | Metaphor and product | Campaign breadth, supporting formats |

**Known flaw.** D2 carries a small diacritic artefact over the I in CLINIC, from model rendered text. It would be reset in production, where the type is typeset rather than generated.
