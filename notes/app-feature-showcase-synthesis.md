# What this means for Pura

Read alongside `../OOH-Superapp-Moodboard/02-pura-ooh-creative-brief.md`. That brief ended on an open decision. This document closes it.

---

## 1. The question this was built to answer

Pura has seven service areas: PureScore, care plans, online consultations, pharmacy, nutrition, wearables and the AI agent. Seven felt like it might be too many to show at once. The OOH study suggested a ceiling of around four, but only from a handful of examples.

Fifty eight entries later, that ceiling was wrong.

---

## 2. Seven is comfortably safe

**Everything below ten features read at a glance. All forty four of them.** The cliff is between nine and ten, and it is abrupt: above ten, only four of fourteen survive.

Two entries sit at exactly seven, and both work:

- **Nubank Business Model.** Seven services on plain arrows pointing into a central label. No craft to speak of, and it still reads.
- **The GoTo Ecosystem For Daily Life.** Seven services arranged around a **clock face**, each pinned to an hour: order a ride at 9am, lunch at 12pm, a parcel at 2pm, a train ticket at 3pm, a car home at 5pm, bills at 9pm, a donation at 10pm.

The GoTo execution is the more interesting of the two and the more relevant, because health services also map naturally onto a day.

**Pura's seven is not a problem to be solved. It is comfortably inside the range where this works.**

---

## 3. The grid is what scales

Round 2 sharpened this considerably. Above ten features, almost everything that still reads is a **grid**:

| Above ten and legible | Count | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Revolut Hub** | 20 | A dedicated Hub screen that is nothing but the service grid |
| Apple devices and services | 13 | Two labelled rows, hardware above, services below |
| Careem app grid | 12 | The real in app service grid |
| Apple Health Browse | 10 | Ten coloured category tiles |
| Starling categories | 10 | Ten coloured spending categories, shown beside the screen they live in |

Revolut is the most instructive. Faced with more products than any competitor, its answer was not a cleverer diagram. It was to **build a Hub screen whose entire job is to be the grid**, and then photograph that. Twenty services, and it reads, because a grid is one object.

What fails above ten is everything that stays a collection of separate things: Gojek's seventeen spokes, Grab's eighteen loose icons, Inc42's sixteen scattered logos, a sphere of forty icons.

## 4. What actually breaks legibility is flatness, not count

The four survivors above ten features are the proof:

| Entry | Features | Why it survives |
|---|---|---|
| Careem app grid | 12 | It is a **real app grid**, a pattern the eye parses as one object rather than twelve |
| Apple Health Browse | 10 | Ten category tiles in a familiar two column grid |
| Paytm | 12 | Split into **two branches of six** either side of the phone |

And the failures at similar counts:

| Entry | Features | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Gojek | 17 | Seventeen **plain ungrouped spokes** off a central logo |
| Grab inventory | 18 | Eighteen loose icons in rows |
| Google Health | 10 | Ten metric tiles around a score, all competing with the score |

Now compare what the strongest examples actually carry:

- **Rappi**: 8 ring segments carrying roughly 20 sub brands
- **Revolut**: 8 ring segments carrying roughly 30 products
- **Tinkoff**: 8 ring segments carrying roughly 40 services
- **WeChat**: 6 colour wedges carrying dozens

All four show **far more than ten things** and all four read, because the viewer is asked to count buckets, not items. **Grouping beats counting.** The number that matters is not how many features you have, it is how many objects you ask the eye to hold at once.

---

## 5. The health finding

Commerce dominates this collection, as it did the OOH one. But the health entries say something the commerce ones cannot.

Round 2 added Apple's health surfaces properly, and they matter most of all.

**Apple Watch's health radial** puts the watch at the centre with ten health icons ringed around it: ear, lungs, apple, blood vial, bed, activity, clipboard, ECG, virus, medication. Ten clinical concepts, arranged, legible, and it does not read as a list of ailments. It is the single most transferable structure in the collection for Pura's wearable surface.

**Apple Health's Browse screen is the closest existing analogue to a Pura service grid.** Ten clinical categories in a coloured tile grid: Activity, Body Measurements, Cycle Tracking, Hearing, Heart, Medications, Mental Wellbeing, Mobility, Nutrition, Respiratory. It reads as a **competent index**, not as a list of things wrong with you, and the reason is framing. The screen is titled **Browse**. It is a library you are exploring, not a diagnosis you are receiving.

That directly answers the risk flagged in the OOH brief. A clinical service grid does not automatically read as burden. It reads as burden when it is framed as a **problem list** and as capability when it is framed as a **library**.

The counter example is in the set too: a concept graphic titled "10 Features To a Successful Mental Health App" lists ten items in a flat chain, one of which is literally **Miscellaneous**. That is what a feature list looks like when nobody has decided what it means.

---

## 6. The single most useful reference: WHOOP

**WHOOP's three score rings are the closest thing in the collection to what Pura should build.**

Three scores, Sleep Performance 81 percent, Recovery 75 percent, Strain 13.2, and beneath each one the **inputs that produced it**: hours versus needed, sleep consistency, sleep efficiency, sleep stress. Heart rate variability, resting heart rate, respiratory rate.

This is not a service menu at all. It is **breadth as inputs converging on an answer**, which is precisely the direction the OOH brief recommended and could not evidence at the time. WHOOP evidences it.

Compare Google Health, which puts ten metric tiles **around** a central score. Same ingredients, opposite result: the inputs compete with the score and the number loses its authority. WHOOP puts the inputs **beneath** the score, subordinate to it. Position carries the hierarchy.

**For Pura, PureScore is the ring and the other six services are what feed it.** That is one dominant object plus supporting detail, not seven peers competing.

---

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Stop treating seven as a problem.** It is inside the safe range, twice evidenced at exactly seven, and nothing below ten failed anywhere in the collection.
2. **If Pura ever needs to show everything at once, build the grid and photograph it.** Revolut's Hub is the proof: twenty services legible because they form one object. A Pura service grid would sit at seven, well inside what Careem, Apple Health and Starling carry comfortably.
3. **Group rather than list.** If the detail has to travel, follow Paytm's two branches of six or Rappi's eight segments with sub brands hung outside. Never Gojek's flat spokes.
4. **Lead with PureScore as the single dominant object, with the other services shown as its inputs.** WHOOP is the reference. This also satisfies the focus group finding already on record, *"I don't need more data, I need better decisions"*, because inputs converging on a score IS a decision, whereas a service grid is more data.
5. **If a full service view is needed, frame it as a library, not a checklist.** Apple Health's Browse is the model, and the framing word does the work.
6. **Consider the day as an organising device.** GoTo's clock is the most human structure in the collection, and health maps onto a day more naturally than ride hailing does.

### Back into the OOH brief

That brief left the decision as *screen or no screen*. The answer this collection supports: **a screen, but a single dominant one.** Not a service grid, and not the fanned multi screen lockup either. PureScore large, its inputs subordinate beneath it, and the left hand message promising the decision rather than counting the services.

The no screen typographic route (the Uber word list) remains worth testing in parallel, because it survives Dubai's letterbox formats where a portrait phone render will not. That constraint is unchanged.
