# What this means for Pura

Read alongside `../OOH-Superapp-Moodboard/02-pura-ooh-creative-brief.md`. That brief ended on an open decision. This closes it.

Built from 80 references, each carrying two independent verdicts: does it read on a screen, and does it survive half a billboard at distance.

---

## 1. The question, and the answer that replaced it

The collection started to answer: **how many services can you show at once before it stops working?**

That turned out to be the wrong question for a hoarding.

Screen legibility does fall off a cliff at ten features. Billboard viability does not. It declines gently, 80% at one to three features, 69% at four to six, 52% at seven to nine, 48% above ten. **Pura's seven services are not the problem.**

The real predictor is **how the thing is drawn**:

| Treatment | Billboard viable |
|---|---|
| Photographic | **100%** (11 of 11) |
| 3D render | **86%** (18 of 21) |
| Illustration | 57% |
| Real UI composite | 50% |
| Flat vector | **26%** (7 of 27) |

**Photographed and rendered objects survive distance. Flat vector diagrams do not.** That is the single most decision relevant finding in either collection, and it is not what the brief expected to find.

---

## 2. The whiteboard sketch is the weakest available structure

The whiteboard drew a ring: specialists converging into a phone, facilities radiating out of it. That structure is `radial`, and it is the most collected arrangement in the set at 22 entries.

**It is also the worst performer. 36% billboard viable.**

Every metaphor led direction beats it outright:

| Direction | Billboard viable |
|---|---|
| container-spill, object-metaphor, mosaic-gestalt, world-place, replacement-stack | **100%** |
| orbit-3d (objects floating in depth) | 88% |
| radial (ring of labelled services) | **36%** |
| flat-diagram | 25% |

The ring fails for a consistent reason: it needs labels, labels need type, and type dies at thirty metres. Four independent health sources drew the whiteboard's exact shape, which confirms it is the convention in health. It is not the same thing as it being effective.

**This does not mean the sketch was wrong.** It means the idea is right and the execution should not be a diagram.

---

## 3. The four structures that actually work

### Container. One familiar object holds everything.

**ERA Real Estate's suitcase is the strongest reference in the collection.** An open suitcase packed with an entire neighbourhood, hospital, school, shops, greenery, under the line *a wide range of facilities*. Three executions, same object, a different world inside each.

It communicates a dozen services with no icons, no grid and no diagram. It is photographic, so it survives distance. And because the object stays constant while the contents change, it is a **campaign system**, not a single execution, which is what seven services need.

For Pura the container could be a medicine cabinet, a kit bag, a doctor's bag, a home.

### Gestalt. Many parts composing one shape.

**Capsule's heart, built entirely from pill shapes.** At distance you see one heart. Up close you see it is made of medicines. It resolves the whole tension at once: one object and many services simultaneously, no compromise between them.

For Pura the shape could be a body, a heart, or the score ring itself, composed of the things that feed it.

### Assertion. Show nothing.

**ClickUp's "One app to replace them all"**, a real transit poster with no interface at all. Careem does the same locally with *The everything app*. This is the cheapest and safest route, and Careem already owns it in this market.

### The solved template.

**"It's everything [brand], in your pocket"** appears in five brand variants: message left, six named service chips, phone right. It is the whiteboard layout, already deployed, at almost exactly Pura's service count. Directly adaptable.

---

## 4. The health finding stands, and sharpens

Every breadth demonstration in commerce is a menu of things to buy. Health cannot borrow that framing directly, because a fanned grid of clinical services reads as a list of problems rather than a set of capabilities.

Two references show the way out:

- **Apple Health's Browse screen** carries ten clinical categories and reads as a competent library, not a diagnosis, because it is framed as *Browse*. The framing word does the work.
- **WHOOP's three score rings**, each with its contributing inputs listed beneath it, is breadth as **inputs converging on an answer** rather than services fanned out.

Halodoc ships the whiteboard's ring for real, with fourteen specialists. Alodokter does it with five large high contrast discs, and Alodokter is the one that survives distance.

---

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Stop treating seven as the constraint.** It is not. Treatment is.
2. **Do not build the ring.** The whiteboard's structure is the weakest performer in the collection. Keep the idea, change the execution.
3. **Build a container or a gestalt.** Photograph or render a single object that holds Pura's services, or compose one shape out of them. Both scored 100%, both are photographic or rendered, and both give a repeatable campaign system rather than one image.
4. **Lead with PureScore as the dominant object, with the other six services as its inputs.** WHOOP is the reference. This also satisfies the focus group finding already on record, *"I don't need more data, I need better decisions"*, because inputs converging on a score is a decision, whereas a service grid is more data.
5. **If a full service view is ever needed, frame it as a library, not a checklist.** Apple Health's Browse is the model.
6. **Two directions are unoccupied.** Nobody in 80 references uses a many armed character or a multiple exposure. Both were searched directly and returned nothing. If Pura wants to be distinctive rather than correct, that is where the open ground is.

### Back into the OOH brief

That brief left the decision as *screen or no screen*. The answer this collection supports: **neither, as posed.** The strongest executions are neither an app screen nor bare typography. They are **a photographed object that contains the offer**. That route survives Dubai's letterbox formats, needs no legible UI, and carries seven services without asking anyone to count them.
